\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Simulation only.} No result here was obtained on a physical robot. Sensors are synthetic, with
randomised noise, latency and dropout, and the fragility cues (vision material, tactile stiffness) are idealised.

\paragraph{Absolute performance is far from industrial.} On arm lots, good parts per lot in round~5 are at most 0.24 for
every policy, including the labelled oracle, and episode success is at or near zero. The gantry is
the only embodiment with high absolute rates. The skill layer, not physical knowledge, is the binding
constraint on the arms, and after repairs several families still have oracle success at or below 0.5.

\paragraph{No win over probe-then-act on arms.} The learned system has not beaten a simple scripted
probe-then-act baseline on any arm in good parts or success. Its demonstrated advantages are over a
generic-memory model, over never probing, in probe count, and on the gantry under a shared budget.

\paragraph{Typed versus generic is not settled.} In prediction and in free planning (rounds~2--3) typed and
generic memory were indistinguishable. The consistent typed advantage appears in the lot experiments. In
round~4 that advantage did not depend on lot memory (the ablation matched), so part of it reflects the typed
model's per-call parameter choice rather than memory across parts.

\paragraph{Statistical power.} Most cells have $n = 30$; intervals are wide, there is no correction for
multiple comparisons, and the ceiling audit uses 6--10 seeds per cell. Single-cell findings should be read as
provisional.

\paragraph{Pre-registration scope and errata.} The sprint and rounds 1--3 were exploratory. Registered rounds
have recorded errata: the round-5 test seed numbers had been used in round~3 under a different protocol
(evaluation only; no training or tuning used them); the \sysI/\sysII{} registration initially hashed code from
a working tree contaminated with a development version of the environments, corrected before any test episode
by pinning a frozen tree with a guard; and a data-sanitising step had silently dropped every held-out-robot
row from an offline evaluation (no reported number used it).

\paragraph{Held-out robot.} All Gen3 numbers in rounds 2--5 were measured with a self-collision defect in the
robot model and understate that arm. Some Gen3 episodes also hit a wall-clock guard because of simulator
instabilities and were counted as failures.

\paragraph{Environment changes between rounds.} The environments were revised between rounds (fragility cues,
site dressing, skill repairs, an easier bin geometry), so absolute numbers are not comparable across rounds.

\paragraph{Pretrained core.} Whether pretraining helps is untested so far: the sprint comparison was not
compute-matched and had no random-initialised control. The controls are built but their results are pending.

\paragraph{Value of the latent.} In many families the gap between knowing and not knowing the hidden physics
is small (at most $+0.08$ on the synthetic world), which bounds what any memory can add there.
