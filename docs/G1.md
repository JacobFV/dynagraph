## Scene (`physmem/robots/g1.py::build_scene_spec`)

- Robot: Menagerie `unitree_g1/g1_with_hands.xml` @ f3b6013, unmodified on disk. Modifications are applied
  through MjSpec at load time and listed in `src/physmem/robots/assets/README.md`.
- **The pelvis is fixed.** The freejoint is removed and the pelvis is welded at z=0.793. An orange visual
  mount shows this, and the video header and overlay say "fixed-base simulation" and "PELVIS FIXED".
- Table: top at z=0.74, x in [0.20, 0.70] m and |y| ≤ 0.42 m in front of the pelvis. Floor plane.
- Object: a dynamic box (freejoint, gravity, box collision geom). Half extents xy 3.5–5 cm, z 2–2.5 cm.
  Boxes this short cannot tip under mu ≤ 0.7.
- Hidden per episode: friction U[0.3, 0.7] (box geom with priority 1, so mu sets box↔table) and mass
  log-U[0.2, 2.0] kg. These ranges match w2 in `worlds.py`.
- Target region: a mocap cylinder with r = 3 cm. It is visual only (contype/conaffinity 0, density 0).
- Rail variant: two static rails, 4 cm high, with a 5 cm gap. The box spans the rails and can be grasped
  from its ±y faces. A static tray (floor plus 1.2 cm walls) is next to it.
- Physics: dt 2 ms, implicitfast integrator, elliptic cone, condim 3 on the box (4 on the hand: soft-finger
  torsional friction). Hand↔box friction is 0.3, the same as the w2 pusher.

## Actuator tuning

The official model has kp=500 and dampratio=1 on every joint. Changes:
- Position servos with dampratio 1 and kp of leg 500, waist 800, shoulder 600, elbow 500, wrist 150,
  hand 15.
- Hand frictionloss lowered from 0.3 to 0.02 and armature to 0.002, because the official values stall the
  finger servos.
- gravcomp=1 on robot bodies, acting as the feed-forward gravity term.

Measured: pusher-site lag (commanded vs true) during a push is 0.6–1.3 cm mean and ≤ 2 cm max at
0.3 m/s. No numerical instability was seen in any run in this document.

## Controller stack

- **Actor boundary:** `G1Sim` (privileged) owns MjData. The actor gets only `ActorPort`, whose public surface
  is time, episode_id, encoders, command, advance and drain_events. The predictor gets only
  EventTensors/QueryTensors. `tests/test_control_attribution.py` (6 tests) checks this.
- **IK:** damped least squares (λ=0.05) on a separate robot-only MjModel, using emulated encoders (16 bit per
  turn plus 5e-5 rad noise).
  - Tasks: pusher-site position, plus a full hand orientation: flat open hand, fingertips down, back of
    the hand parallel to the contacted face, leaning ∈ {0.3, 0, −0.3} along the push direction.
  - Joint set: waist yaw/pitch plus 7 arm joints, with a null-space posture pull.
  - The arm (left or right) and the lean are chosen by IK feasibility on four waypoints (transit, start,
    mid-stroke, end), with a 0.8 cm tolerance.
- **Push primitive** (`G1Robot.execute_push`). It takes ActionCandidate "push" (θ, φ, v, T) in the object
  frame of the decision-time pose estimate, with the same geometry as `worlds._push_geometry`.
  1. Lift both hands vertically to transit height (table + 20 cm) and park the other arm.
  2. Joint-space approach to the IK solution at transit height above the pre-contact point.
  3. Descend at 0.4 m/s to box top + 7 cm, then wait until encoder FK is within 6 mm.
  4. Descend at 0.15 m/s to the pre-contact point: 3.5 cm above the table, 1.5 cm effective hand radius
     + 2.5 cm gap outside the boundary (w2 uses 3 mm). Wait until encoder FK is within 6 mm (≤ 0.6 s).
  5. Straight-line push at v for T.
  6. Immediate release: withdraw 3 cm along −d at 0.6 m/s, so the box slides freely.
  7. Lift.

  A velocity-feedforward variant (target + (kv/kp)·q̇) was tried. It made fast strokes worse (overshoot,
  realized/predicted 1.35), so it is off.
- **Grasp/reach/lift/place:** `G1Robot.lift_place`, a two-hand flat-palm squeeze. This is the rail variant;
  it did not pass the gate.
- **Observation adapter (mirrors `data.py`):**
  - Object POSE2: synthetic noisy sensor, σ 2 mm / 1°, 20 Hz.
  - Object VEL2: derived causally from consecutive pose measurements.
  - Object SIZE: once, σ 2 mm.
  - Hand POSE2: forward kinematics of the encoder joints at 20 Hz. It follows the `data.py` sphere-pusher
    convention exactly: yaw reported as 0 + N(0, 0.5°), position noise 0.5 mm, arrival latency
    U(0.2, 1.0) ms, provenance SENSOR.
  - Hand SIZE: 1.5 cm (sphere15).
  - Own COMMAND: emitted whenever it changes.
  - CONTACT: synthetic tactile on the hand entity, 10 Hz, thresholded hand↔box normal force (0.5 N) plus
    noise and 2 % flips.

  Entity ids use the `obj_`/`pus_` prefix convention. No MASS or FRICTION events are emitted.
- **`control.py` (shared by every model):**
  - Candidates: 48 per decision. Speed is log-uniform from 0.05 m/s to the capability maximum (0.8 m/s);
    duration is 0.1–0.5 s (the dataset v2 range). Feasibility is checked against the capability workspace.
    In slide-to-target, 40 % of candidates are goal-directed FAST pushes (0.45–0.8 m/s), and every
    candidate respects an 8 cm hand stroke (v·T ≤ 0.08 m).
  - Prediction: one batched predictor call per decision.
  - Scoring: `|goal − world(mean[h=2.0 s])| + λ·sd + c_push·1[pred dist > 3 cm]`, with **λ = 0.5**
    (fixed, logged).
    - Easy benchmark: sd = sqrt(var_x + var_y) and c_push = 0.
    - Slide-to-target: sd = sd of the predicted DISTANCE to the goal (variance projected on the goal
      direction) and **c_push = 0.05 m per anticipated extra push**.
    - Model variances are used as-is, with no per-model calibration.
  - IK fallback walks down the ranking (up to 64 candidates).
  - Stopping: the loop ends when the belief pose is within 1.5 cm of the goal and the object is still, or
    when the push budget is used (easy 6 / slide 3), or at the sim cap (easy 20 s / slide 15 s), or when
    no candidate is feasible.
  - Logs: every candidate's score, the chosen id, belief revision/time, `memory_updates`
    (WorldBelief.snapshot diff), predicted vs realized at each horizon, contact events, and inference
    latency.
  - Tensors are built with `data.events_to_tensors`.
  - Predictors are built with `make_predictor`: `stub[:mu]`, `analytic`, `cv`,
    `compact:<ckpt>[:passes]`, and `oracle` (labelled ORACLE, true friction, eval-only).

### Main benchmark: slide-to-target (`results/g1/slide30`, seeds 0–29)

**Design.**
- Target 15–30 cm from the box, along a direction whose direct push was checked to be IK-reachable at
  sampling time.
- Target centre inside x∈[0.30, 0.62], |y| ≤ 0.34.
- Budget 3 pushes, 15 s cap.
- Episode objective J = final distance + 0.05·n_pushes.
- Success uses the push criterion: within 3 cm, slower than 2 cm/s for 0.5 s, clean audit.

| predictor | success | mean / median final dist | pushes | J | dist after 1st push | fast pushes chosen | latency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| stub μ=0.5 | 14/30 | 5.0 / 3.4 cm | 2.77 | 0.189 | 10.3 cm | 58 % | 1.1 ms |
| analytic | 8/30 | 6.2 / 4.5 cm | 2.80 | 0.202 | 10.3 cm | 46 % | 31.6 ms |
| ORACLE | 15/30 | 5.5 / 3.2 cm | 2.67 | 0.188 | 10.3 cm | 59 % | 1.2 ms |

**Slow-only policy check** (`slide30_slowonly`: capability max speed 0.3 m/s, same seeds). Only **4/30
(13 %) succeed**, with both ORACLE and the stub (mean final 16.7 cm, all 3 pushes used). With the full
speed range, 14–15/30 succeed. So fast pushes are necessary on this benchmark. 46–59 % of chosen pushes
are fast (≥ 0.45 m/s), so the distance-only uncertainty penalty does not force slow pushes.

**Interpretation.** ORACLE barely beats the μ=0.5 stub (15 vs 14/30, J 0.188 vs 0.189). The cause is G1
execution noise:
- For ORACLE fast pushes (n=47), predicted-vs-realized displacement along the push direction has mean
  +0.4 cm and **sd 4.2 cm**; lateral error is 1.3 cm.
- This error is uncorrelated with μ (r = −0.11).
- Speed tracking (0.83–1.16×) and stroke overshoot vary with arm configuration, and slide goes as v². At
  these strokes, this scatter is larger than the effect of friction (a few cm).
- A predictor that learns the G1's own execution statistics from proprioception could do better.

The analytic baseline has two separate problems. Its friction estimates get worse after G1 pushes:
|μ̂−μ| is 0.09 at k=0 (prior), 0.26 at k=1 and 0.37 at k=2, while its reported sd is ≈ 0.02
(overconfident). Its least-squares fit assumes w2 release timing and is biased by the lag of the G1 hand.
It also uses its uncalibrated variance as-is.

`results/g1/slide_video_s29/` has paired videos for seed 29 (μ=0.32): ORACLE succeeds (2.4 cm), the stub
misses (3.6 cm), analytic misses (4.8 cm). The videos include the magenta ORACLE overlay.

### Controller-v2 attempt (bounded, 05:56–06:10Z): NOT adopted, v1 kept

Goal: reduce the scatter in fast-push execution. Adoption criteria, over 50 calibration strokes:
- release-speed ratio (true hand speed along d over the last 3 stroke ticks ÷ v_cmd) within ±5 % at
  p10–p90;
- standard deviation of the oracle along-push error (true-μ stub prediction vs realized) < 2 cm.

Calibration strokes use **training-calibration seeds 10000–10049 only** (the test seeds are 0–49). This
is embodiment-adapter calibration. Each stroke is one goal-directed push: speed log-U[0.1, 0.8], stroke
≤ 8 cm; 46 of the 50 were feasible. Script: `scripts/demo_g1_servo_calib.py`. Raw results are in
`results/g1/servo_calib/*.json` and `summary.json`.

What was tried (`G1Robot.push_stroke_servo`, flag `g1.PUSH_SERVO="v2"`):
1. Task-space speed servo: IK target = measured (encoder-FK) position + v·lookahead + KP·(s_des − s_meas)
   + KI·∫(v − v_meas). Every gain set tried was unstable or stalled. With lookahead 0.02, KP 0.2 and
   KI 1.0, the release ratio p10–p90 was 0.79–1.37 and the error sd 6.9 cm. Without KP/KI the hand
   stalls under load (median ratio 0.65).
2. Measured-progress stroke: the reference runs at v from a run-up point 2–6 cm behind the pre-contact
   point (so the hand is up to speed before contact), and the stroke ends when MEASURED progress reaches
   the commanded length. Options tried: PI on measured speed, a bound on reference lead (anti-surge),
   and an early stop lead.

| controller (n=46 strokes) | release ratio p10 / p50 / p90 | along err sd (all) | sd excl. 1 outlier | sd fast (≥0.45 m/s, n=15) | fast-push ratio p10–p90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v1 (current) | 0.79 / 0.99 / 1.14 | 2.9 cm | 2.0 cm | 3.2 cm | 0.78–1.12 |
| v2 run-up 4 cm | 0.96 / 1.00 / 1.14 | 2.8 cm | — | 2.1 cm | — |
| v2 run-up 6 cm | 0.96 / 1.00 / 1.14 | 2.9 cm | 1.9 cm | 2.0 cm | 0.97–1.12 |
| v2 run-up 6 cm + lead bound 2 cm | 0.93 / 0.99 / 1.13 | 2.7 cm | 1.7 cm | 2.0 cm | 0.85–1.07 |
| v2 run-up 4 cm + lead bound 1.5 cm | 0.74 / 0.98 / 1.10 | 2.5 cm | — | 2.2 cm | — |

**Decision: not adopted.** Neither criterion is met. The best p90 is 1.10–1.14, outside ±5 %. The
overall error sd is 2.5–2.9 cm, above 2 cm.

What v2 does improve:
- On fast pushes, error sd drops from 3.2 to 2.0 cm.
- The release-ratio tail narrows: p10 rises from 0.78 to 0.97.

What remains:
- The p90 tail comes mostly from 0.2–0.45 m/s strokes, where the ratio reaches 1.19–1.20. The hand surges
  when the load drops at contact loss.
- Fast pushes now show a +1–2 cm *bias*: the box is launched faster than the hand (impact). This is
  model mismatch of the simple Coulomb stub, not scatter.
- One stroke out of 46 (seed 10002, slow push) knocks the box backwards 13.5 cm in both controllers. This
  is an approach-geometry failure; it alone takes the error sd from about 1.7–2.0 to about 2.7–2.9 cm.

Per the rule, the default stays `PUSH_SERVO = "v1"`, so all slide30/easy30 results above stand, and
`demo_g1_compact_paired.py` uses v1. The v2 code is kept behind the flag.

## e15 adapter dataset "g1cal" (G1 embodiment, PhysSplit format)

Generator: `scripts/demo_g1_make_g1cal.py`. Output: `~/dynagraph/data/g1cal/{train,val,test}_tensors.pt`,
`*_params.jsonl`, `manifest.json`, `scales.json`. Load with `physmem.data.load_split("train"|"val"|"test", "g1cal")`.

- **Seeds and splits:** roots are slide-to-target episodes on calibration seeds 20000–20599 (train),
  20600–20699 (val) and 20700–20799 (test). Root id = 9000000 + seed. The split is made by root before
  windowing. These seeds are disjoint from the G1 test seeds 0–49 and the servo-calibration seeds
  10000–10049.
- **Pushes:** up to K=4 per root. Each is a uniformly chosen IK-feasible candidate from the
  slide-to-target candidate distribution (fast_frac 0.4, stroke ≤ 8 cm, duration 0.1–0.5 s), executed by
  controller v1. Hidden friction is U[0.3, 0.7] and mass log-U[0.2, 2] kg.
- **What a sample is:** sample k is what the G1 controller sees at decision time.
  - Input: the actor event stream (the same stream fed to the predictors), tensorized with
    `data.tensorize_sample` at the decision time `t_dec`, which is before the approach.
  - Action: action k.
  - Label: the future box state at `t_push + HORIZONS`, in the canonical frame of the actor's latest pose
    estimate at `t_dec`.
- **Label definitions:** labels follow `data.make_labels_np`. Ground truth is recorded at 100 Hz on the
  evaluator side.
  - contact_mode comes from the hand↔box force.
  - free_slide_mask covers the time after stroke end + 0.05 s with no contact.
  - law = 0.
- **Horizon coverage:** the robot holds until `t_push + 2.1 s` after each stroke, so every horizon is
  observed.
- **Scales:** not refit. `scales.json` is a byte copy of `data/v2/scales.json`.

## Embodiment differences vs. the training data (w2)

- Hand POSE2 follows the w2 sphere convention (yaw 0 + noise); SIZE is reported as sphere15 (1.5 cm).
- Pre-contact gap: 2.5 cm (+1.5 cm effective radius) vs 3 mm in w2. Contact starts ~0.03–0.5 s after the
  commanded push start, depending on speed.
- Approach time: ~1.5–2.5 s of arm motion between the decision and the push start (transit, descent,
  convergence waits), vs a 0.25 s re-position in w2. The object is static during this time.
- Pusher: a flat, wide open hand (~8 cm across) at 3.5 cm height, vs a sphere at 2 cm.
- Speed tracking is 0.83–1.16× (the w2 velocity-servoed pusher is ≈ 1×).
- Release: a 3 cm back-off at 0.6 m/s.
- COMMAND is emitted on change (start/end of the stroke), as in w2.
